# Experiment report — 6 real DotBots, PIBT step-by-step

**Date:** 2026-06-15
**Script:** `draft_real_dotbot_pibt.py`
**Setup:** 6 DotBots · map 2000×2000 mm · 8×8 grid · cell = 250 mm · 30 steps max ·
arrival threshold = 100 mm · per-step timeout = 8.0 s

## Goal

Run PIBT on real hardware in a *dense* configuration (small cells relative to robot
size) and measure how many robots actually reach their goal. This serves as a metric
showing that, at a 2000×2000 map size with an 8×8 grid, the DotBots run into trouble in
large numbers.

## Result — 3 / 6 reached their goal

| Bot | Goal | Outcome | Notes |
|-----|------|---------|-------|
| 15763f18 | (1, 2) | ✅ reached (step 3) | short, conflict-free path |
| 217b8297 | (0, 6) | ✅ reached (step 6) | clean traversal |
| 5a26b4d4 | (1, 7) | ✅ reached (step 7) | clean traversal |
| 35cd1796 | (1, 0) | ❌ lost | never approached goal; oscillates (6,0)↔(6,1) until step 30 |
| c61037a3 | (6, 0) | ❌ lost | stuck near (6,0)↔(5,0), repeated step timeouts |
| e91cbc5a | (6, 1) | ❌ lost | oscillates (6,1)↔(6,2) for the rest of the run |

The three failures share a signature: from roughly step 6 onward they stop making
progress and bounce between two adjacent cells, repeatedly tripping the 8.0 s
per-step timeout (`⚠ not arrived before timeout`). The three robots that reach their
goal do so early (by step 7) and then stay parked while the rest of the swarm churns.

## Interpretation

PIBT itself produces a valid, collision-free *plan* on the discrete grid. The failures
are physical, not algorithmic: a 250 mm cell is too tight for a DotBot to perform the
half-turn it needs to reach the next waypoint. The robot overshoots the 100 mm arrival
threshold, the step barrier times out, the next replan sends it back, and it ends up
trapped in a two-cell oscillation. Because the bottom-right corner of the map
concentrates three goals (35cd1796, c61037a3, e91cbc5a all route through column 6 / the
bottom row), the contention there is worst — and that is exactly where all three
failures occur.

## Comparison across configurations

| Grid | Cell size | Robots | Result |
|------|-----------|--------|--------|
| 4×4 | 500 mm | 4 | ✅ works — cells large enough for the half-turn |
| 8×8 | 250 mm | 5 | ❌ fails — collisions from insufficient turning space |
| 8×8 | 250 mm | 6 | ❌ 3/6 lost (this run) |

**Takeaway:** the limiting factor at a fixed 2000×2000 map size is **cell size vs. the
robot's turning footprint**, not the number of robots per se. Coarser grids (larger
cells) leave room for the half-turn and succeed; the 250 mm cell of the 8×8 grid does
not, and the failure rate grows with density as goals pile up in the same region.

## Possible follow-ups

- Increase cell size (coarser grid) or enlarge the map so each cell exceeds the turning
  footprint.
- Relax the 100 mm arrival threshold and/or raise the per-step timeout to absorb
  overshoot instead of replanning into oscillation.
- Spread goals to avoid concentrating contention in one corner.
